*Artisanal intelligence, mass-produced.*

[**McElixr**](https://spicemine.iotj.cc/ui/iotone-oss/McElixr) is a new IoTone project, first published on November 20 in our [space on Spicemine](/news/2026-11-23-iotone-oss-on-spicemine/). It is two things at once.

## The joke

McElixr is a parody AI company. You choose an elixir from the menu (Synergy-4 Turbo, Hallucinate Pro), pour a prompt through it, and get back a paragraph of confident buzzwords on a till receipt, with a token count and a price. The landing page promises "enterprise-grade synergy in every prompt" and adds: "No card required. No intelligence required either."

![The McElixr Brew page: a prompt form, the elixir menu, and answers printed as receipts](/images/news/mcelixr-brew.png)

No model is called. The default "barista" writes the buzzwords itself.

## The serious part

Every business web application needs the same foundation before it can do anything of its own: people sign in, belong to organisations, hold roles, and may or may not be allowed to do each thing. Someone has to be able to see who did what. That work is slow to do well and easy to get subtly wrong.

McElixr is that foundation, finished and tested, with a silly product on top to prove it works. It is written in **Elixir** with **Phoenix LiveView** and **PostgreSQL**. To build something real, you replace one module (the brewing one) and keep the rest.

| Area | What is there |
|---|---|
| **Sign-in** | Email and password, or a magic link sent by email. Confirmation, remember-me, and sign-in throttling. |
| **Account types** | Solo (one person), team (a workspace with members), and partner (an agency with a book of clients). Platform staff are a fourth kind and cannot self-register. |
| **Roles** | Owner, admin, builder, analyst and billing in a workspace. Each role maps to a list of permissions. |
| **Tenant isolation** | Every data call carries the caller's scope, so one organisation cannot read another's rows. |
| **Audit trail** | Append-only. It names the member, their role and their organisation, and records identifiers, never content. |
| **Session security** | An idle timeout each organisation sets (5 to 60 minutes) with a warning banner, and a re-check of the password before sensitive actions. |
| **API** | A JSON API under `/api/v1` with API keys that act as the member who created them, and a WebSocket for live updates. |
| **Privacy** | Download my data as a ZIP, and account deletion with a 30-day purge. |
| **Staff area** | Accounts, organisations, the audit log and a support inbox. Staff never see tenant content. |
| **Languages** | English, Latin American Spanish and Japanese, each with its own date order. |

![The Team page: members, their roles, and pending invitations](/images/news/mcelixr-team.png)

### Permissions are checked in three places

A permission is checked where the data is read or written, again before a page opens, and again when the sidebar is drawn. A builder does not see a Billing link, cannot open the Billing page by typing its address, and could not change the plan even if the page were shown by mistake.

The demo data makes this easy to see. It ships ten accounts, one for each role: sign in as the analyst and you get read-only history, sign in as billing and you get the plan and usage with no brews.

![The Organization page: the idle timeout setting and the audit trail](/images/news/mcelixr-organization.png)

### Accessibility is tested, not hoped for

The portal targets WCAG 2.1 AA. An automated scan opens every page as every role and fails the build on a violation. The "Night Lab" theme comes in light and dark.

## Run it

You need Elixir 1.17 or later and PostgreSQL.

```sh
git clone https://spicemine.iotj.cc/iotone-oss/McElixr
cd McElixr
mix setup            # dependencies, database, demo data, assets
mix phx.server       # http://localhost:4000
```

The sign-in page lists the demo accounts. No email is sent in development: sign-in and invitation links appear in a local mailbox page. There is also a Nix shell, and a Dockerfile with compose files.

### Make the elixirs real

To connect a real model, write one module with one function (`pour/2`) and name it in the configuration. Nothing else changes.

## What to know first

The README says plainly that this is a starter and not a finished product:

- The legal pages are placeholders.
- The Spanish and Japanese translations are machine-made and have not been reviewed by a native speaker.
- The container image and compose files have not been built or run yet.
- Nothing is charged. There is one marked place where a payment provider would go.
- There is no two-factor sign-in or single sign-on yet, and a user belongs to one organisation.
