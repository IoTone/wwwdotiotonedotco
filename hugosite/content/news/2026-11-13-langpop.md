[**langpop**](https://github.com/IoTone/pop-11-langpop) is a new language tutor from IoTone, opened on October 30. It teaches two languages: **Japanese** for a Western learner, with a teacher called Aoi, and **English** for a Japanese test-taker, with Mr Hill. It runs on our [Poplog](/projects/poplog/) port and has taken well over a hundred commits in its first two weeks.

## The curriculum is a graph

Most language apps are a deck of cards and a schedule. langpop keeps two separate things:

- **What it knows about the language** is a Prolog graph: items, and the order they have to be taught in.
- **What it knows about you** is a set of numbers that changes every time you answer a question.

The prerequisites do the teaching. A word requires the kana that spell it, so the first words open when your kana are solid, not on a date a syllabus picked. Leave the prompt empty and the tutor asks the next thing you are ready for and weakest at. Ask `:why` about any item and it shows its working: what the item requires, where the fact came from and where you stand on it.

```
Aoi>
  い    ->  o
  Close. Look again at the shape.  That is お -- the two are confused constantly.

Aoi> :why hi_ka
  か  (grapheme, preschool, own(kana))
    ka
    missing(hi_a)  missing(hi_i)  missing(hi_u)  missing(hi_e)  missing(hi_o)
    belief 0.0 on channel read (mastery is 0.8)
```

Both ladders begin *below* the bottom of the official one, because that is where learners begin. Someone with no hiragana is not a beginner at N5. They are below it, and langpop has a pre-school rung for them.

## No server, no model, no network

There are two front ends over one engine:

- **One process.** The graph is Prolog, the student record and terminal interface are Pop-11, and nothing leaves the process.
- **One protocol.** The same engine behind JSON-RPC, where the terminal becomes a client with no privileges a phone app would not have.

There is no language model anywhere in it. Every answer you give is appended to a plain-text journal, one line per answer, so the audit of your progress is a file you can read with `grep`.

## What is in it

A fresh clone holds 1,309 items: hiragana, katakana and the first fifty words, enough for several weeks. Importing the freely available JMdict and KANJIDIC2 reference files takes eight commands and brings the Japanese ladder to **6,212 words and 1,023 characters, N5 through N2 reading in full**. The English track is written for a reader of Japanese and includes loanword traps and the sound contrasts that Japanese speakers find hardest.

The last two weeks added:

- **Sound.** `--audio` renders a level with the system voice, so listening questions have something to play. English and Japanese are both covered.
- **`:drill`**, timed practice that counts for nothing.
- **`:progress`**, the last 28 days as one mark a day:

```
  studied    ··········█······█··██·█████   9 of 28 days
  right      ··········▇······█··▁█·██▇█▇   94%  ↑ up 2 points on the week before
```

- **Things to print.** A kana chart on one page, and a booklet to fold.
- **Per-language progress.** Reaching N5 in Japanese says nothing about your English, so each language keeps its own rung and its own reviews.

## Try it

```sh
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IoTone/poplog/master/tools/install-skill.sh | sh
git clone https://github.com/IoTone/pop-11-langpop
cd pop-11-langpop
LANGPOP_POPLOG="$HOME/.local/share/pop11-skill/poplog" ./langpop --user you --lang ja
```

The single-process tutor works with the current Poplog release. The JSON-RPC server and client need a library newer than the latest binary release, so for now those need a Poplog built from source. langpop checks at startup and tells you which it needs.

The README is candid about limits: the graph is short of words above N2, and the speaking check only measures one thing so far. Source and design notes are on [github.com/IoTone/pop-11-langpop](https://github.com/IoTone/pop-11-langpop).
