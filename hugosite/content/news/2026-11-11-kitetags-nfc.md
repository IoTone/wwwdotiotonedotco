In case you have ever been curious about NFC and weren't sure where to start, this is the place. One of our customers, [Tagstand](http://tagstand.com), has launched [Kitetags](https://kitetags.com), a no-code solution for NFC tag application development. No apps to install. Just order your tags, configure, and you are ready to tap. Created with the awesome design work of David Cisneros, you can get a fleet of physical IoT tags deployed in quantities of 1–1000 in under 5 minutes.

[Start here](https://docs.kitetags.com/docs/getting-started/quickstart/) (hint: you can get a tag for free if you follow the link to the free stuff).

As someone who has worked with NFC and Tagstand (YC S11) since they sold to the new owner, I can tell you that the process of getting started with NFC on your own is complex. Sure, you can buy tags directly and set them up with NFC Writer or any of the other NFC tools. It's tedious. How do you fix mistakes once out in the field? You don't. You can't. Until now. With [Kitetags.com](https://kitetags.com) you are able to modify the application on the fly, track analytics, and take tags in and out of production.

Have more questions? You can hit up Tim and the team at [tagstand.com](http://tagstand.com). If your project has complex tag requirements (security badges, specialized labels, brand marketing), or you just need stickers, reach out to the team directly.
