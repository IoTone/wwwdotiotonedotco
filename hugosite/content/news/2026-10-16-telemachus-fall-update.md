Two months after [opening Telemachus](/news/2026-08-11-telemachus/), our self-hosted, privacy-first AI lab for teams, the repo has absorbed roughly a hundred commits of platform work. The highlights:

## The worker fleet, seen and managed

Federated compute grew an operational face: the **Workers card** shows the fleet, a host can be **drained** before maintenance, a worker declares whether it is taking work, and a **reaper** cleans up abandoned jobs — and leaves a record when it does. The queue is inspectable job by job, running work can be **canceled**, run time is **capped**, and a deferred job now says *which* limit is holding it.

## Typed judgments

The largest new seam is the **typed-judgment system**: an LLM judge evaluates work and returns a typed verdict rather than prose — and that judgment is a first-class object. It is **kept**, it **bills its own dimension** against the team's AI quotas, and it flows over a typed wire with its own chat backend. The beta judge was validated against the seam before any of it self-hosted.

## Layered operations and hard-won fixes

A civil-engineering RFE drove a run of platform gaps closed under one banner: workflows that **wait for a person**, team-defined object metadata with extraction provenance, plugins that can claim a file format, out-of-process plugins reaching the document repository, capability records and a decision chain. Alongside: offboarding and password hygiene, an enforced upload byte ceiling on the S3 data plane, a guarantee that a raw `DATABASE_URL` never reaches a log, and an end-to-end gate that can no longer report green against a server it never booted.

## Japanese, cover to cover

The product's own translation engine finished the job on its documentation: the remaining eighteen chapters of the Telemachus e-book are now in **Japanese**, joining the fully localized UI. The feature-tour screenshots in the README remain generated by Playwright tours on every push — including the new fleet and judgment surfaces.
